      Four Inputs, Three Engines

      Recording and uploading audio both feed the same voice engine, and video and image both feed the same visual-artifact engine. Each engine is a separate open model doing one job — none of them share a verdict with another.

      AUDIO

      Voice

      Listens to a live recording or an uploaded clip and scores it for the acoustic fingerprints of synthetic speech. Most reliable on clips between 2.5–13 seconds.

      model: wav2vec2-deepfake-voice-detector
      VIDEO

      Video

      Samples frames evenly across the clip (recorded or uploaded) and checks each one for visual artifacts common to face-generation and face-swap models, then averages the result.

      model: Deepfake-Detect-Siglip2
      IMAGE

      Image

      Runs a captured or uploaded image through the same visual-artifact model used for video frames, checking for face-generation and face-swap fingerprints.

      model: Deepfake-Detect-Siglip2 (shared with Video)
      TEXT

      Text

      Reads pasted text for the statistical patterns of language-model output. Needs 25+ words to give a reading worth trusting.

      model: xlm-roberta-text-detector
      // EVIDENCE STANDARD

      Read This Before You Trust a Verdict

      • This is a signal, not proof. Every verdict is a probability from a model, not a confirmed fact — treat it as one input among several, not a final answer.
      • Short or noisy clips are less reliable. Very short audio, low-frame-count video, and sub-25-word text all widen the margin for error — the tool flags this when it happens.
      • False positives happen. Plain, simple, or heavily-edited human writing and audio can occasionally read as synthetic. A single flag is a reason to look closer, not to conclude.
      • Files aren't kept. Uploaded audio and video are deleted from the server immediately after analysis; nothing is stored or reused.
      // FAQ

      Common Questions

      What file types can I upload?

      Most common audio formats work for the audio input (wav, mp3, m4a, webm — browser recordings default to webm). The video input accepts standard containers like mp4 and mov; if a file won't open, re-encoding to mp4 usually fixes it. The image input accepts jpg, png, and webp.

      Why did it flag my own writing as AI-generated?

      Text detectors like this one are the least reliable of the four inputs — they're known to misfire on plain, short, or simply-structured writing regardless of who wrote it. Treat a text flag as a prompt to double-check, not a verdict on its own, especially under 25 words.

      Is a "likely AI-generated" result something I can use as proof?

      No. These are probabilistic classifiers, not forensic tools with legal standing. Use a result to decide whether something deserves closer scrutiny — not as evidence on its own.

      Does anything get saved or sent elsewhere?

      Uploaded audio and video are written to a temporary file for the length of the analysis and deleted immediately after, whether the analysis succeeds or fails. Text inputs are processed securely in memory.

      Which models power each input?

      Voice runs on wav2vec2-deepfake-voice-detector, video and image both run on Deepfake-Detect-Siglip2, and text on an xlm-roberta text-detector — all open models, listed in full in the footer below.
